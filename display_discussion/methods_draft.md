Methods

Study system and data

We used haul-level data from the ICES North Sea International Bottom Trawl Survey (Maureaud et al., 2024), combined with reconstructed trawling effort (otter and beam, summed) from Couce et al. (2020). For each haul we calculated species richness (S), total abundance (N), and total metabolic energy (E = Σ n·mass^0.75 across length bins, normalised by the haul's minimum observed individual mass, m_min^0.75), the state variables required by the Ecological Equation of State (EEoS; Harte et al., 2022), alongside observed community biomass (B_obs).

H1 asks whether the EEoS predicts haul-level biomass from state variables alone, with no free parameters, better than chance. Our primary test compared unfitted EEoS predictions, B_pred(S,N,E), against B_obs. Because a strong test of a parameter-free theory requires bracketing its performance against both an uninformative floor and an informed ceiling, we report this alongside a benchmarking envelope: a null model permuting biomass to destroy any S/N/E–biomass relationship (floor); an unfitted productivity relationship, log(E × m_min) against log B_obs, using each haul's minimum observed individual mass as the calibration term (competitor); and a fitted log-linear model of biomass on E (ceiling). This structure evaluates whether the EEoS's advantage over a simpler productivity relationship justifies the additional theoretical machinery, not simply whether it beats chance.

As a sensitivity check on the interpretation of any absolute discrepancy between predicted and observed biomass, we tested whether the relative structure of the EEoS is recovered independent of absolute scale, following the E/B^(3/4) relationship of Harte et al. (2022, Fig. 2).

H2 and H3 test whether EEoS residuals — defined as log(B_obs) − log(B_pred) throughout, held constant across every model reported below — carry a systematic fishing-pressure signal, spatially (H2) and temporally (H3). Fishing pressure was decomposed at the ICES statistical rectangle level (n = 158 rectangles, 10,464 hauls) into a between-rectangle term, the haul-weighted mean of log(trawling hours + 1) within each rectangle, and a within-rectangle term, each haul's deviation from that rectangle mean. We fitted a mixed model, residual ~ FP_between × phase + FP_within × phase + (1 | rectangle), with a fishing-pressure regime classification, phase_v2, anchored to known EU fisheries policy transitions (1992 and 2002 Common Fisheries Policy reforms; the 2008 Long-Term Management Plan/MSFD), yielding four periods (1985–1991, 1992–2001, 2002–2007, 2008–2015). We report H3 temporal contrasts directly from this model.

Because between-rectangle effects are subject to neighbour autocorrelation that an exchangeable random intercept does not capture, we refitted the identical fixed-effect structure with a proper conditional autoregressive (CAR) spatial term over a queen-contiguity rectangle adjacency matrix (free spatial correlation parameter), and report H2 spatial contrasts from this model; each model's estimate of the other's effects serves as a spatial-family sensitivity check on the corresponding hypothesis. A reduced model omitting the FP_between × phase interaction was used to confirm the interaction, rather than a constant spatial effect, was required to detect a significant signal. We applied a Bonferroni correction (m = 4) across the four phase-specific H2 slopes to control family-wise error.

We assessed two further sensitivities against the primary specification. First, we tested whether spatial fishing-pressure effects reflect confounding with the spatial arrangement of fishing itself, via 1,000 global permutations of rectangle-level FP_between with the model refit on each permuted dataset. Second, we refitted the primary specification on 1,000 random subsamples of rectangles at each of 80%, 60%, and 40% of the full spatial coverage (without replacement), assessing stability as the proportion of subsamples in which the sign, and separately the sign and significance (p < 0.05), of the phase-specific contrast matched the full-sample estimate.

Separately, we probed whether the spatial signal reflects spillover of fishing pressure from neighbouring rectangles rather than local pressure alone, a distinct question from whether the signal is present. We refitted the primary specification adding a k-nearest-neighbour (k = 4) lag of neighbouring rectangles' FP_between, interacted with phase, and report this alongside the primary local-effect coefficients as evidence on the spatial scale over which fishing pressure operates, rather than as a robustness check on H2 itself.
